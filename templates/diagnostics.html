What each cell costs the business

False negative An order predicted as not late that arrives late. No mitigation is triggered, the customer is surprised, and service recovery happens after the fact. This is the expensive error for a service business.
False positive An order predicted late that arrives on time. Cost is a needless mode upgrade or an unnecessary proactive notification — real, but bounded and knowable in advance.
True positive Correctly flagged late order. This is where the decision engine can act: upgrade the mode, or warn the customer before they chase.
True negative Correctly cleared. No intervention, no cost.

Because false negatives cost more than false positives, recall matters more than precision here. At the default threshold the model recalls {{ '%.1f'|format(100 * m.recall) }}% of genuinely late orders. The slider below shows exactly what raising recall costs in false positives — and the decision page prices that trade-off in dollars rather than leaving it as a judgement call.

Youden's J is a diagnostic, not the business-optimal threshold. It weights a false positive and a false negative equally, which they are not. The economically justified operating point comes from the expected-cost model on the Decision Optimization page.

Threshold simulator

Do the probabilities mean what they say?

The decision layer multiplies predicted probabilities by dollar costs, so it needs probabilities that are accurate in level, not merely correctly ranked. A model can have excellent AUC and still be badly calibrated.
