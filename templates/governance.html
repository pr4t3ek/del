Model governance

Limitations, assumptions and what would need monitoring if this model were deployed.

Limitations

Data and inference

  • Correlation is not causation. Every association reported here is descriptive.
  • Shipping-mode assignment is observational. Historical data does not identify the causal effect of switching mode. Counterfactual scores are model-implied scenarios, not predicted outcomes of an intervention.
  • No freight-cost data exists. The dataset has no carrier, warehouse or logistics-cost field, so total logistics cost cannot be measured. All cost figures rest on stated assumptions.
  • Late delivery has no measurable profit impact in this data. The correlation is {{ analytics.profit_vs_lateness.correlation }}, so the penalty for lateness cannot be calibrated from the data and must be assumed.
  • Post-outcome variables cannot be used ex ante. Excluding them costs a great deal of apparent accuracy, and that is the correct trade.
  • The final year is partial. {{ analytics.eda.partial_final_year.year }} contains only {{ '{:,}'.format(analytics.eda.partial_final_year.orders) }} orders.

What this dataset can and cannot answer

The delay process here is close to mode-determined. Realised transit time depends on shipping mode and very little else: with mode removed, all {{ ab.without_mode.n_features }} remaining operational and external factors together reach {{ '%.3f'|format(ab.without_mode.metrics.roc_auc) }} AUC against a chance level of 0.500. Second Class and Standard Class have statistically indistinguishable transit distributions and differ only in what they promise.

Conclusions about which factors drive delay therefore describe this dataset's structure. They should not be read as a general finding that geography, product or seasonality never affect real-world delivery performance — only that they do not do so here.

What does transfer. The methodology — leakage screening, order-grain testing, effect sizes alongside p-values, and pricing the reliability/speed trade-off explicitly rather than optimising late rate alone — applies to any real delivery dataset.

Privacy. Although email and password are masked placeholders, the source file still carries customer names, street addresses and per-row geocodes. It is shaped like a re-identifiable customer table and should be handled accordingly. All such fields are excluded from every model here.
