The decision chain

1. Predict

What can we forecast?

Late_delivery_risk from information available at the moment the shipping-mode decision is made. The preferred model ({{ analytics.best_model }}) reaches {{ '%.3f'|format(analytics.model_comparison[analytics.best_model].roc_auc) }} ROC-AUC on held-out data.

2. Decide

What can management change?

Shipping Mode — the one lever in this dataset that is both controllable and materially associated with delivery performance. Every order is scored under all four modes and compared on expected total cost.

3. Optimize

What is the economic outcome?

Order Profit Per Order against a transparent expected-cost model. The best policy tested lowers expected cost per order by {{ '%.1f'|format(d.policies.policies[0].saving_pct) }}% versus a single-mode baseline.

Late rates track the promise, not the operation

Promised window vs actual transit time, by mode

Second Class and Standard Class move goods at effectively the same speed, but Second Class promises {{ d.promise_redesign.second_class.promised_days }} days against Standard's {{ d.promise_redesign.standard_class.promised_days }}. That single difference in commitment accounts for a {{ '%.0f'|format(100 * d.promise_redesign.late_rate_reduction) }} percentage-point gap in late rate.

Scenario assumptions, not observed values. The DataCo dataset contains no freight-cost, carrier or logistics-cost field, so total supply-chain cost cannot be measured from it. Every monetary parameter in the decision layer — freight cost per mode, cost of a late delivery, holding rate, value of delivery speed — is an analyst assumption that you can change on the Decision Optimization page. Results are reported as ranges and stability regions rather than single numbers.
